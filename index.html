x goes from −6 to 6, so a whole x² square (up to 36 balls) still fits on a phone.

Step 1

Why stop at 36? Every number can be laid out as one long row, 1 × n. With 36 balls that row already shrinks each ball to about a third of its size on a phone, and rectangles like 2 × 18 are too long to build by hand on the screen. Bigger numbers work exactly the same way, so try them on paper.

How the tiles work

  • Ball, rectangle, squareThe ball is 1. The rectangle is x: one ball wide and an unknown length long. The square is x²: x long on each side. Red pieces are negative.
  • Add and subtractTap + next to a tile to add one. What − does depends on the ± buttons switch above the tray. Count: − takes a tile away (or adds a negative one if there's nothing to take). Zero pairs: − adds the negative tile, which cancels a positive one as a zero pair. The switch works the same in Expression and Equation mode. You can also drag a tile, + or − onto the board to place it exactly.
  • Zero pairsA tile and its opposite add up to 0. Drop a ball onto its opposite (x onto −x) and both disappear. Cancel pairs does every pair at once. The Zero pairs demo shows why they work.
  • Move, turn, flip, removeDrag to move; pieces snap together when they're close. Tap an x tile to stand it upright. Double-tap to flip a piece's sign. Drag to the bin at the bottom to remove.
  • Suggested approachAfter you Build an expression or equation, for example one from your homework, a card suggests a way to tackle it, with a button to start. Tap Suggest any time to ask again.
  • Try a value for xTap Try x = and pick a number. Each x tile turns into a row of that many balls and each x² tile into a square of them, so you can count the value, or check whether both sides of an equation balance.
  • Complete the squareIn Expression mode, with one x² tile and an even number of x tiles on the board, tap Complete the square. Your own tiles move step by step into a square, with zero pairs added if the corner needs them.
  • EquationsAdd to Both sides to keep the balance. Dragging a ball across the = sign flips its sign. Divide shares both sides into equal groups. If the balls don't share evenly, the leftovers are cut into slices, so an answer like x = 3½ shows as 3 balls and half a ball.
